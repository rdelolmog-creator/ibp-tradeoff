SNAKE PROMPT — STEP 7, OPTIMAL-POLICY MODEL
IBP Trade-Off Engine · Week 6 Capstone · AI Finance Accelerator

Step 7 of the §5 build order, redefined at D-064 and architecture v4. The
previous definition — "learn cost response to schedule disruption" — is not
built. A model learning cost from engine output would be learning the engine,
which is circular and §9 territory.

What Step 7 does instead:

  (a) sweep the engine per SKU for the cost-minimising parameter set. This is
      DETERMINISTIC and it is the operational answer.
  (b) fit SKU ATTRIBUTES to those optimal parameters, benchmarked and explained
      with SHAP, so a policy can be set for a SKU the engine cannot sweep —
      principally a NEW LAUNCH, which has no demand history and therefore no
      Step 5a characterisation, but does have every attribute the model needs
      before its first shipment.

The optimisation is deterministic. Machine learning explains and generalises
the policy; it does not produce it. That sentence belongs in the pitch and on
the limitations slide.

Prerequisite: Step 6 gate passed (D-051, restated on the revised engine at
D-060). Step 8 (broadening to all lines) and the O-10 review (D-063) come
after.

PART 1 — the deterministic sweep (no ML)
-----------------------------------------
In src/policy_model.py, class PolicyOptimiser:

  __init__(self, engine, line_id)
      Takes an already-constructed TradeOffEngine. Does not rebuild it.

  sku_grid(self, sku_id, cover_values, service_values) -> pd.DataFrame
      For one SKU, evaluate the cost surface over the Cartesian product of
      inventory_cover_weeks x service_target, holding min_run_hours at the line
      default. Returns one row per grid point with the four economic outputs
      and the total.

      IMPORTANT — the per-SKU isolation problem. Conversion cost is a LINE
      property: changeovers and capacity are shared across the SKUs on a line,
      so a single SKU's cost is not separable from its neighbours'. Two
      admissible treatments, and the choice must be stated not assumed:
        (i)  hold every other SKU at the line default and vary one SKU at a
             time, attributing the DELTA in line conversion cost to that SKU;
        (ii) exclude conversion cost from the per-SKU objective entirely and
             optimise the three separable outputs.
      Implement (i) as the default and expose (ii) as a flag. Report both in
      the notebook — if the chosen optimum differs between them, that is a
      finding about the separability of the objective and must be logged, not
      smoothed over.

  optimise(self, cover_values, service_values) -> pd.DataFrame
      Runs sku_grid for every SKU on the line, returns one row per SKU:
      sku_id, optimal_cover_weeks, optimal_service_target, total cost at the
      optimum, cost at the line default, and the saving between them.

      Flag any SKU whose optimum sits at a grid EDGE. An edge optimum means the
      true optimum is outside the searched range and the value is not a real
      optimum — it must not be fed to the model as if it were.

  Grid resolution comes from assumptions.levers.*.range, not from literals.
  Use a grid, not a continuous optimiser: the surface is not smooth (min-run
  rounding and the excess threshold both create steps), so gradient methods
  would find artefacts.

Write optimal_policy.csv with, per SKU:
  sku_id, line_id, optimal_cover_weeks, optimal_service_target,
  total_cost_at_optimum_eur, total_cost_at_default_eur, saving_eur,
  edge_optimum_flag, assumption_fingerprint


PART 2 — the policy model (ML)
-------------------------------
In src/policy_model.py, class PolicyModel:

  Targets  : optimal_cover_weeks, optimal_service_target (two separate models)
  Features : category, abc_class, gross_margin_eur, price_eur, std_cost_eur,
             shelf_life_days, case_size, moq_units, min_run_units,
             line_speed_units_hr, irreducible_volatility_cv, chronic_bias_l1

  Two feature sets, fitted and reported separately — this is the point of the
  step, not an extra:
      FULL   : all features above
      LAUNCH : excludes irreducible_volatility_cv and chronic_bias_l1, because
               a new launch has no demand history and therefore neither. This
               is the set that will actually be used in the launch case.
  Reporting only FULL would overstate real-world performance.

  fit(self, X, y, feature_set) -> fitted estimators
      Two estimators per target: an XGBoost regressor and a linear benchmark
      (ridge, with categorical features one-hot encoded). The benchmark is not
      a formality — with 60 rows and largely categorical features, the linear
      model may well win, and that is a legitimate and reportable result.

  evaluate(self) -> pd.DataFrame
      Leave-one-out cross-validation, NOT a train/test split — 60 rows will not
      support a holdout. Report MAE and R2 per target, per feature set, per
      estimator, plus a naive baseline (predict the ABC-class mean) so the
      reader can see whether either model beats "use the class default".
      If neither beats the naive baseline, SAY SO. That is the honest result
      and it is more informative than a tuned number.

  explain(self) -> SHAP values
      SHAP on the winning estimator per target. The deliverable is the planner-
      readable statement: which SKU attributes drive a SKU toward high cover,
      and in which direction.

  predict_launch(self, attributes: dict) -> dict
      The operational output. Takes a new SKU's known attributes, returns
      recommended opening cover and service target using the LAUNCH model.
      Must refuse, not guess, if a required attribute is missing.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E — EXTRA DETAILS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- No hard-coded paths, SKU lists, line ids, category names or rates. Grid
  ranges read from assumptions.levers.*.range.
- src/engine.py is NOT modified. Step 7 consumes it.
- sklearn, xgboost and shap are confined to src/policy_model.py and must not
  become import-time dependencies of the engine or the app's critical path.
- min_run_hours is deliberately excluded from the per-SKU optimum: it is a LINE
  decision, and changeovers are a property of the sequence on a line, not of
  any one SKU. forecast_bias_correction is excluded too — it is a planner-
  accountability lever, not a per-SKU policy parameter.
- Every artefact carries the assumption fingerprint. A policy optimised under
  one assumption set is not valid under another.
- Determinism: the sweep has no RNG. The ML step must seed everything and
  report the seed.
- Two limitations to carry into Step 13, stated in the notebook and not only in
  the code: (a) the most predictive features are exactly the ones a new launch
  lacks, so LAUNCH performance is the honest number and FULL is the optimistic
  one; (b) a policy rule fitted on 60 SKUs is defensible as method, not as a
  number.
- Nothing is final until it has run in Colab and the numbers have been
  confirmed by the reviewer's own run (D-037).
